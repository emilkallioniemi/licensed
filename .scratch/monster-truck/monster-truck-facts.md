# Monster-truck factual grounding

Checked 2026-09-12. Bounded research for the cooperation candidate; this does not decide the role split.

## Facts and firsthand observations

- Monster Jam describes trucks approximately 10.5 feet tall, 12.5 feet wide, 17 feet long, and 12,000 pounds. Their tires are 66 inches tall. They use four-link suspension and nitrogen-charged shocks with 30 inches of travel. Drivers often sit centrally inside a protective roll cage. These are competition vehicles, not enlarged road pickups. [Monster Jam 101](https://www.monsterjam.com/en-gb/monster-jam-101/)
- Four-wheel drive and four-wheel steering are real features: the official game's developers describe studying these mechanisms with the truck reference team. [Monster Jam Showdown development diary](https://monsterjamvideogame.com/how-to-build-a-monster-truck/)
- In his firsthand 2018 training account, Ezra Dyer operated rear steering through a separate toggle beside the wheel. He found its response delayed and learned to anticipate it. His harness and head restraint severely limited looking around. These observations concern his training setup, not every truck. [Car and Driver firsthand account](https://www.caranddriver.com/features/a23807573/monster-trucks-monster-jam-university/)
- Throttle has effects beyond speed: Monster Jam defines a “power out” as acceleration used to avoid rolling over. Its glossary also describes bouncing wheelies and balancing on front tires. [Monster Jam glossary](https://www.monsterjam.com/en-gb/monster-jam-lingo/)

## Design implications, explicitly inventions

- Splitting front steering, rear steering, and throttle/brake among three people adapts real control channels into fictional teamwork. Real operation does not require three drivers.
- Blind spots have a factual basis, but giving each player exclusive views is a game design choice. These sources do not establish exact blind-spot geometry around ordinary parked cars.
- Bouncing and tilting can amplify mistimed controls, but perpetual low-speed instability is an arcade invention. Long suspension travel alone does not establish uncontrollable bouncing.
- A throttle/brake role could have satisfying timing decisions and recovery moments; that is a candidate to test, not evidence everyone will find the role fun. Rear steering likewise need not be a passive helper role.
- Do not copy simulation complexity merely because it exists. Even the official game's developers describe changing bounce between stadium and outdoor handling to serve playability. [Monster Jam Showdown development diary](https://monsterjamvideogame.com/how-to-build-a-monster-truck/)
