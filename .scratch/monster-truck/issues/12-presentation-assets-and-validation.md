## Answer

Resolved with the user on 2026-09-13 through two question rounds. This is the asset-production and integration plan for the complete vehicle, not a claim that assets or gameplay validation are finished.

### Production direction and ownership

Astra owns substantial model, rig, animation, effects, audio, and integration work. Give Astra free hands on the learner's design: the existing body, proportions, and appearance are not constraints. Retain the agreed scope of one expressive learner body and rig, three distinct clothing colours, matching license portraits, and no customisation screen. The complete vehicle keeps the chunky, stylised industrial direction and exaggerated, initially gore-free slapstick agreed in [What presentation makes the monster truck feel complete?](05-finished-experience.md).

Reuse existing materials and production patterns where useful, without making them visual requirements. Blender 5.2.1 LTS was verified locally. Existing generators, Blender source files, GLB exports, and previews establish a practical production path. Keep editable source assets and generation scripts; integrate portable exports into Godot with materials, collision shapes, pivots, animation state transitions, and appropriate audio resources. Existing movement and multiplayer code is a foundation, not proof that new rigs or truck interactions work.

### License and emote handling

Use a dedicated toggle for the physical license while the learner's hands are free. Show the printed face to nearby friends and provide a readable view to its owner. Taking a driving control or starting an emote puts it away; it cannot be shown while operating a driving control. Keep the accepted hold-to-open, release-to-perform five-choice emote wheel. Exact key assignment can be fitted alongside the control-guidance decision; it does not change these interaction rules.

### Examiner and audio coverage

- Brace on hard jolts. Allow at most one puke and two muttered swears per attempt, with at least 30 seconds between distress outbursts. These limits apply to outbursts, not every bracing movement. Scored comments take priority. Tune driving-event thresholds in play so violent driving motivates the reactions.
- Provide three alternative comments per fault category in the agreed route/scoring plan, plus arrival, all five manoeuvre requests, pass, fail, timeout, and concession coverage. Avoid repeating an alternative until its category is exhausted. Keep dry assessments distinct from physical distress and respect the existing aftermath-before-results flow.
- Use offline rendered speech WAVs: the existing Windows System.Speech pipeline produced the examiner announcement, while the recorded ready-up diagnosis identifies crashes in runtime Godot TTS. Existing procedural music generation uses Python/numpy; verify its runtime before production. No dedicated audio/music generation tool was exposed during inspection.
- The user does not require exceptionally polished audio. Use available generation methods and iterate for suitable character and intelligibility; do not make access to a premium audio service a prerequisite. If a specific sound cannot be produced adequately with available tools, identify that concrete dependency during implementation instead of silently omitting it or assigning all production to the user.

### Checkpoint coverage and final acceptance

The first cooperation checkpoint remains the bounded rough implementation described in [How will the first playable build validate three-player cooperation?](06-cooperation-playable-comparison.md).

The complete playable test checkpoint includes decent Astra-produced Blender models for the full route, truck, people, and active machinery, plus generated audio for driving, hazard cues, and examiner-directed progression. Provide enough working animation and accident presentation to understand the test and evaluate its aftermath. These are real, revisable production assets. Full emote, radio, detailed animation, visual-damage, and presentation coverage belongs to the finished vehicle and must be complete before final acceptance; gameplay-driven changes can revise earlier assets.

During integration, verify Godot imports, materials, collisions, rig/animation transitions, audio playback, shared-state presentation, and clean retry/return resets. Review a successful test and every agreed serious-accident type with three humans. Finish only when the asset inventory is integrated, hazards are readable from intended learner positions, first- and third-person hands/gestures/card work, accidents have convincing aftermath, and engine/radio/examiner/player voice remain intelligible together. Fix distracting clipping, missing reactions, and intrusive repetition. The user accepted these checks; no such review has occurred in this planning session.

Three-human play and feedback are the known human dependency. Agent-led source creation and integration remain the default. Blender and Godot are located; verify remaining local audio runtimes as implementation setup. Do not invent an external service requirement before a concrete production need appears.

No new decision ticket is needed. Exact control guidance and shared multiplayer behavior remain with their existing tickets. Questions that arise only from future play remain explicitly provisional implementation feedback rather than blocking this specification route.
