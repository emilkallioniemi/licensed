## Question

With candidate roles and test structure in hand, which situations and procedures should players master, and which hazards or instructions should change? Determine how repeat attempts preserve necessary communication without relying on forgotten instructions or arbitrary surprises. Identify the content and variation needed for this complete vehicle without assuming procedural generation.

## Answer

Resolved with the user on 2026-09-12 through acceptance of all five proposed decisions. This is a candidate for playable validation, not evidence that replay already works.

- Keep the route and required manoeuvres fixed initially. Players can learn the scrapyard and improve their rating while changing conditions supply fresh problems.
- Familiar worker and machinery hazards vary through timing and a few authored variations. Each variation must give players time to notice and react, so learning where danger comes from still requires looking and communicating before moving.
- Draw a fresh hazard variation on every attempt, including retries after failure; do not preserve the failed attempt's variation as a practice mode. This does not promise that a random draw can never repeat.
- Start with three encounters with two authored variations each, plus modest timing changes. Keep the remaining course consistent. This defines an initial content target, not a procedural-generation requirement.
- Reset the scrapyard on every retry. Wreckage and physical consequences persist within the current test; each new attempt starts with a usable course.
- Validate through repeated three-player play whether this amount of variation keeps experienced players communicating. The existing cooperation experiment still owns proving that all three players remain necessary; variation alone does not prove that.
