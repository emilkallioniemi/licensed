## Answer

Resolved with the user on 2026-09-12 through the presentation discussion. These are the agreed targets; detailed asset scope and validation remain follow-up decisions.

- Finished visuals use chunky, stylised 3D: oversized machinery, expressive people, readable silhouettes, battered industrial colours, and an operating scrapyard with neglected safety.
- Accidents use violent slapstick: ragdolls, flying objects, crumpled wrecks, dust, and absurd trajectories. Start without gore; the physical incident supplies the shock and comedy.
- The examiner rides in a dedicated passenger seat. Players see him clutch his clipboard and brace; occasional puking and muttered swearing follow particularly violent driving. Spoken assessments remain dry. Exact triggers and frequency require further definition.
- Sound is central: powerful engine, loose-metal rattles, suspension creaks, tyre scrub, cabin clatter, and distinct metal, ground, and machinery impacts. Preserve space for player conversation.
- A physical dashboard radio provides shared speaker playback with power, next-track, and volume controls. Placement was delegated: the nearest control position can reach it, and other players can ask for a change or leave their control and walk over. Exact reach follows the cabin layout; music becomes quieter outside. Begin with calming easy listening, heavy rock, and upbeat dance, several tracks overall, and slight ducking beneath examiner comments and player voice while retaining audible vehicle and hazard sounds.
- Five emotes: wave, clap, point, both middle fingers, and "67". The last alternates both palms up and down with a short "six seven" line once per selection. Hold a key for the five-choice wheel and release to perform; show the player's own gesturing hands in first person. Emotes are unavailable while operating a driving control: release it first.
- Present a short examiner assessment over the aftermath with a marked test sheet showing the shared result, minor faults, and passing rating. Keep the wreck visible behind it. Retry, return, and best-rating persistence remain with [How are results, best ratings, and retries shared?](11-results-and-retries.md).
- First three-player gameplay validation needs rough truck and scrapyard shapes, readable hazards, basic collision sounds, engine feedback, and temporary examiner speech. Finished art, detailed sound, radio, gestures, and examiner animation follow gameplay validation, within the complete-vehicle scope.

Follow-up: [Which presentation assets and checks are required to finish the monster truck?](12-presentation-assets-and-validation.md) defines the bounded asset set and acceptance criteria. Cabin control placement remains with the control-layout decisions. No game implementation was produced.
